# Line 5 source audit

Checked 19 September 2026. Scope: all 22 current stations, English and French. The route runs from Bobigny – Pablo Picasso to Place d’Italie, with one continuous path and no branches.

## Route and method

[Bonjour RATP’s Line 5 page](https://www.bonjour-ratp.fr/en/lignes-metro/ligne-5/) confirms the endpoints and 22 stops. Its [official line map](https://assets-bff.bonjour-ratp.fr/lineMaps/pdf/plan-de-ligne_METRO_RATP_ligne-5_LIG:IDFM:C01375.pdf) was opened to verify the order. The older ratp.fr PDF address was unavailable, so the working Bonjour RATP map is used.

Every station article listed below was opened. The name, history and platform sections were read where needed. Municipal, museum, church and research sources provide further checks for selected claims. These are cited beside each station’s text in the app. Each English explanation starts with the naming reason; each French explanation preserves the same chain. Context adds a separate fact. Short explanations are not padded to meet a word target.

The artwork is an interpretive engraving of the Austerlitz viaduct, not a historical document. No image is used as evidence. No station opening-year field is added. Verified dates remain in the short historical paragraphs.

## Editorial decisions

- Jaurès is a direct tribute to Jean Jaurès. The station was renamed before the avenue, so the explanation does not reverse that order.
- Richard-Lenoir preserves François Richard’s adopted surname, taken in memory of his partner. The municipal garden page loosely describes a joint tribute; the station article explains the specific naming chain. No unsupported industrial priority claim is retained.
- Bréguet–Sabin combines two streets and two distinct references. The Breguet family name has no accent; the street and station retain theirs. No claim about invention of the self-winding watch is included.
- Saint-Marcel’s dragon is explicitly a legend. Its depiction is documented by the Centre André Chastel. The historical name comes through the boulevard and former suburb.
- Austerlitz names a battle, not a railway destination. The railway station, quay and bridge share this commemoration. The text does not assert an unsupported one-to-one naming chronology between them.
- Campo-Formio commemorates the treaty associated with Campoformido. The text does not assert that the signatures were physically applied in that town, which is a separate historical question.
- Jacques Bonsergent’s execution is dated only to December 1940. The app avoids contested priority claims such as “first French civilian” and unnecessary uncertainty about the exact execution site. The Paris municipal archive documents the public execution notice.
- Quai de la Rapée uses the spelling on the official map. No circumflex is added.
- Shared station IDs match the existing entries: `stalingrad`, `gare-de-lest`, `place-ditalie`; `gare-du-nord` is coordinated with Line 4.
- No em dashes are used in the data. En dashes remain in compound station names.

## Validation

- 22 unique station IDs and one ordered path cover every stop once.
- Every station has non-empty English and French name origins and context.
- All source links use HTTPS; all 22 station source articles were opened during the audit.
- Existing shared IDs match Line 7. The Line 4 researcher received the Gare de l’Est ID.
- Scoped TypeScript check passed. Data import and invariants passed. No em dashes in public copy.
- Browser and integrated functional checks are owned by the root task.

Independent cross-review correction: the context now states the 1907 extension to Étoile and permanent transfer to Line 6 in 1942. It does not imply continuous Line 5 service during the temporary 1931 transfer. See [cross-review](../reviews/line5-cross-review.md).
