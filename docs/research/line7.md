# Line 7: name and source audit

Checked: 19 September 2026. Scope: `apps/web/src/data/line7.ts`.

## Route check

The [SNCF Transilien text version of the RATP diagram](https://www.transilien.com/fr/page-lignes/metro-7) lists all 38 stops in order and states that the fork is after Maison Blanche. It agrees with the [Line 7 station list](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligne_7_du_m%C3%A9tro_de_Paris). The [RATP line page](https://www.ratp.fr/vos-lignes/metro/7) confirms the line and links its map.

- Shared section: 29 stations, La Courneuve–8 Mai 1945 through Maison Blanche, inclusive.
- Ivry branch: Porte d’Italie → Porte de Choisy → Porte d’Ivry → Pierre et Marie Curie → Mairie d’Ivry.
- Villejuif branch: Le Kremlin-Bicêtre → Villejuif–Léo Lagrange → Villejuif–Paul Vaillant-Couturier → Villejuif–Louis Aragon.
- Complete paths contain 34 and 33 stops. Each repeats the common section once. No path joins the two southern termini directly.

## Method and limits

Each station article was opened and its name/history section read. Links below support the name claim and the short context, unless a separate supporting link is listed. Wikipedia provides the station-level naming chain. Official sources provide the Gobelins history and the Kremlin-Bicêtre name history. Source text was paraphrased into short French and English paragraphs. Person links are optional further reading, with separate language URLs.

No `opened` field is set. This avoids confusing a station’s first opening on another line with its arrival on Line 7. Selected years in the prose were checked in the individual articles. In particular, the southern stations opened on Line 10 in 1930 and moved to Line 7 in 1931. Month/day claims are avoided except the documented 1 May 1946 opening and 8 March 2007 renaming.

Uncertainty and corrections:

- Cadet: the station source says the name *may* come from the gardener brothers. Both languages retain that qualification.
- Château-Landon: the street article explains an older erroneous attribution to the distant town and the later research identifying a local owner. Both sources are provided; no unsupported owner biography is added.
- Censier: the published street-name derivation is *sans chef*, an old term for a dead end. It is not described as a land-tax collector.
- Jussieu: the named person is Antoine-Laurent de Jussieu. The text does not turn this into a general dedication to every botanist in the family. The station article contains an unrelated error about the date of the Ivry extension; that claim is excluded.
- Les Gobelins: the fifteenth-century origin is a dye workshop. The royal tapestry manufacture came later. The official Mobilier national history takes precedence over the station article’s compressed chronology. No exact year for Jehan’s workshop is asserted.
- Sully–Morland: sources differ on the exact early renaming year. The text says it acquired the compound name soon after opening, with no exact date.
- Tolbiac: no precise battle year is given; this avoids treating the traditional chronology as certain.
- Kremlin-Bicêtre: the municipal history calls the tavern “Le Nouveau Kremlin”; other accounts use other signs. The text states the common naming chain without asserting one exact sign.
- Porte/Place d’Italie: these names refer to a road destination. They do not imply that the railway itself runs to Italy.
- Museum, hospital and municipal names identify the immediate namesake. No speculative ancient etymology for Louvre, Villejuif, Ivry or Aubervilliers is asserted.

## Checks

- All 38 IDs are unique and use ASCII kebab case.
- Every station has both languages for the name explanation and context.
- Each explanation contains 25–55 words; each context contains 30–75 words, counted by whitespace.
- Every station has a source URL. All station source pages were opened during research.
- Every path ID exists. The path union covers all 38 stations. The shared prefix ends at Maison Blanche.
- Branch labels agree with the published station order. Les Gobelins uses `loom` art.
- The module passes a standalone TypeScript check against the shared contract.
- The module includes no live traffic, timetable, fare, or accessibility claims.
