# Line 4 source audit

Checked 19 September 2026. The module contains 29 stations, ordered from Porte de Clignancourt to Bagneux – Lucie Aubrac. All stops have English and French name origins and short context paragraphs. No em dashes occur in the module.

## Route and method

The [RATP line map](https://www.ratp.fr/plans-lignes/metro/4) and [Transilien textual station sequence](https://www.transilien.com/fr/page-lignes/metro-4) establish the route. The [RATP extension account](https://www.ratp.fr/prolongement-metro-ligne-4) confirms the January 2022 extension to Barbara and Bagneux. The Transilien page contains older interchange text; only its station sequence is used.

Station pages were read for the direct naming chain. Institutional, municipal, museum and operator sources supplement them where available. Wikipedia is a secondary source and does not provide archival proof for every early street-name origin. Uncertain dates and disputed origins are qualified or omitted. The copy is an original bilingual summary. Opening-year fields are omitted.

## Station checks

### 1. Porte de Clignancourt

Gate name derives from the former Clignancourt hamlet. Annexation in 1860 and the Puces de Saint-Ouen subtitle checked. No speculative ancient personal-name derivation.

- [Porte de Clignancourt · Wikipédia](<https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porte_de_Clignancourt_(m%C3%A9tro_de_Paris)>)

### 2. Simplon

Station follows Rue du Simplon and the Alpine pass. Napoleonic road and 1906 railway tunnel checked. Omit the disputed road-completion date and precise pass height.

- [Simplon · Wikipédia](<https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_(m%C3%A9tro_de_Paris)>)

### 3. Marcadet – Poissonniers

Both street origins and the 1931 merger checked. Market origin and fish-trader route retained; unsupported medieval precision omitted.

- [Marcadet – Poissonniers · Wikipédia](<https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcadet_-_Poissonniers_(m%C3%A9tro_de_Paris)>)

### 4. Château Rouge

Square derives from the vanished house. Demolition in 1889 retained. Construction is explicitly approximate, following the source; no unsupported claim about red brick or royal ownership.

- [Château Rouge · Wikipédia](<https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_Rouge_(m%C3%A9tro_de_Paris)>)

### 5. Barbès – Rochechouart

Two boulevard dedications checked. Marguerite is identified as abbess of Montmartre, not confused with a male aristocrat. Exact dates of her abbacy vary among sources and are omitted.

### 6. Gare du Nord

- 29 unique station IDs and 29 entries in one continuous path.
- Termini are display names; first and last path IDs match those stations.
- Existing interchange IDs retained.
- All four text fields are between 20 and 45 words for origins, and between 20 and 55 words for context.
- All source and biography URLs use HTTPS.
- Featured stations exist: Saint-Sulpice, Montparnasse – Bienvenüe and Barbara.
- No em dashes, generated-image claims, opening-year guesses or service-frequency claims.
